# Kaklık–Kocabaş quarries (Denizli): site research for modelling

Compiled 2026-11 for the Quarry Maker project. Sources are listed at the end, with Turkish sources marked
**[TR]**. Numbers are quoted with their source; where sources disagree, the range is given.

**What the stone is.** The Kaklık–Kocabaş quarries cut **travertine**, sold as "Denizli travertine" and
commonly called *mermer* (marble) in the trade. Geologically it is not metamorphic marble. It is
layered calcium carbonate deposited by hot springs on fault lines and in shallow lakes, between about
1.6 million and 85,000 years ago. This explains most of what is seen on site: flat-lying beds,
straight saw-cut walls, beige-white colour, porous layers, and clay and gravel layers between the
stone beds.

## 2. Location and setting

- The quarries lie on the south-eastern edge of the **Denizli Basin**, an extensional graben. It
  trends NW–SE and is bounded by the Pamukkale fault to the north and the Babadağ and Honaz faults to
  the south. The basin is famous for the Pamukkale travertines [Toker Tagliasacchi 2018, TR].
- Demirkıran & Çalapkulu (2001) [TR] place their study area **about 35 km east of Denizli**, around
  Kocabaş and Kaklık. It includes the Kömürcüoğlu, Ege Maden, Çakmaklar, Sirmersan, Mayaş, Modül and
  Başaranlar quarries.
- Özpınar et al. (2001) [TR] describe three Kocabaş quarry sub-areas:
  - **Zeytineğrek**, north-east of Acıdere;
  - **Etence Tepe** and **Killik Tepe**, east of Acıdere cemetery.

  Kömürcüoğlu is the easternmost quarry of the Kocabaş field.
- Our area (W29.31 S37.84 E29.39 N37.89) spans 466–946 m. The published "Kocabaş travertine level"
  sits at the lowest ground of the district (around 370 m near Kocabaş, per a search summary of
  the 2013 comparison paper). Our area is higher, so it probably exposes mainly the older, higher
  levels (Killik, Sarıkaya, Taşkestik). **This is my reading of the maps, not a published
  statement;** check it against MTA's 1:25,000 sheet (see §10).

### The four travertine levels (Demirkıran & Çalapkulu 2001 [TR])

| Level, oldest first | Thickness | Colour and character | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Taşkestik** | 4–12 m | whitish beige, very even lamination; plant traces reduce its value | upthrown fault blocks: Etence, Kepez, Taşkestik and Düğdüllü hills |
| *detrital layer* | ~20 m | greenish-white and brown clays, conglomerate, thin travertine beds | Killik Tepe, Naldöken |
| **Killik** | 4–30 m | light beige, very even lamination; darker with Fe/Mn stains near faults | Ege Maden, Çakmaklar, Alimoğlu and Kömürcüoğlu quarries |
| *detrital layer* | 17–34 m | conglomerate, finely bedded green clays, brown clay, 1 m gastropod bed | between Killik and Sarıkaya |
| **Sarıkaya** | 4–40 m | lightest colour, many plant remains; thickens to the south | Başaranlar, Denizli Çimento and Modül quarries |
| **Kocabaş** | 2–15 m | white, softer than the older levels | lowest ground: Kocabaş, Kocaçukur, Kocadüz |

- **Thickening on faults.** Where Killik and Sarıkaya merge without the clay layer between them
  (Domuz Deresi, north of Ege Maden, Düzçalı Sırtı), the stone reaches **80–90 m**. There the
  laminae steepen to 30–45° and contain in-place breccia.
- **Toward the basin.** To the south the levels get more clay-rich; to the north they pass into sand
  and gravel. The levels formed at the basin edge.

**For the model.** A quarry wall here is not one uniform rock face. It is a stack of white-beige
travertine beds a few metres to tens of metres thick, between softer marl, clay and conglomerate
bands. Quarries open where a thick, clean bed (5–25 m) comes near the surface, and they stop at
clay bands. This is why bench floors are flat and level across large areas: **floors often follow a
bedding plane, not an arbitrary elevation**.

## 4. Landforms that formed the stone (Demirkıran & Çalapkulu 2001 [TR])

| Type | Geometry | Kocabaş examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Vein (banded)** | vertical crack filling, bands 1–2 cm parallel to the walls, hard, often aragonite | **Kocabaş vein: 2.2 km long, N60°W, dips 85° S**; Mayaş vein 500 m long, 12 m thick, E–W, dips 85° S; Mayaş B 30 m further north |
| **Fissure ridge** | elongated mound along the crack | 500–1000 m long; a Kocabaş fissure ridge **~2.7 km long, up to 300 m wide, ~20 m high**, trending WNW–ESE, exposed in two active quarries; vertically banded travertine ~16 m thick on inactive ridges |
| **Fault-front** | on the downthrown block of a normal fault | south of the Kocaçukur–Kekikli fault |
| **Terrace** | arc-shaped pools on slopes, softer, grey from weathering | Kocaçukur, small areas on ridges |
| **Channel** | M-shaped walls along a flow channel | around Haydarbaba sinkhole (Kaklık), Güneyyatak Tepe |
| **Lacustrine (lake)** | very even lamination with regular gas pores; the main quarried type | all four levels |

Gürlek–Kocabaş lithotypes (Toker et al. 2015; Toker Tagliasacchi 2018 [TR]):
- laminated, coated gas-bubble, reed, paper-thin raft, intraclast and extraclast (pebble) travertine;
- paleosol layers of 10–60 cm (dark brown-grey, organic);
- the stone formed in marsh-pool and flat-pool settings (a shallow lake);
- laminae are horizontal and continuous for hundreds of metres;
- U/Th ages run from 231 ka to 85 ka.

At Kömürcüoğlu (Özpınar et al. 2001 [TR]):
- the quarried stone is dense white **shrub-type** travertine in **5 levels**, of which levels 3 and 4
  from the top are thick and continuous enough for blocks;
- **brown, porous reed-type travertine on top is waste** that must be stripped. Its thickness drives
  the cost;
- gravelly travertine sits at the base of the stack.

## 5. Faults, joints and earthquakes

- **Directions.** N60°W and E–W faults dominate, with local N–S faults. Travertine follows the E–W
  and N60°W cracks, and the cracks inside the Killik level run the same way. The faults are
  horst-and-graben normal faults [Demirkıran & Çalapkulu 2001, TR].
- **Fault ages at Kömürcüoğlu.** NW–SE and E–W faults are older; NNW–SSE faults are younger and
  shaped the area after the travertine formed [Özpınar et al. 2001, TR].
- **Throws.** At Düzçalı, a series of E–W normal faults dipping south, each with 5–10 m throw, offset
  the Killik and Sarıkaya levels by **~100 m in total**. Fault zones are crushed and brecciated, with
  Mn staining. Faults on Etence–Taşkestik dip 80° south; the north blocks are up.
- **Effect on quarries.** "Quarries opened near the fault zone are heavily fractured; some were
  abandoned for this reason" (Zeytineğrek). Quarries away from tectonic lines are the economic
  ones (Etence, Killik Tepe) [Özpınar et al. 2001, TR]. One Kaklık quarry reports faults running from
  the surface to the floor, with thin green streaks in the faults [Çalışkan & Yavuz 2019, TR].
- **Regional faults.**
  - The **Honaz fault** is an active E–W normal fault about 15 km long bounding the basin to the
    south.
  - Segment names include an E–W Kaklık segment and a NE–SW Kocabaş segment.
  - Reported vertical slip is about 0.14–0.15 mm/yr, and earthquakes of magnitude 6 or more are
    possible.
  - Kocabaş is at the west end of the Honaz fault zone.
- **Hazard.** The 2019 national hazard map gives 475-year PGA ≈ 0.3–0.9 g across the region. The
  8 Aug 2019 Bozkurt earthquake (Mw 6.0, AFAD) was nearby. Take Honaz/Kaklık-specific values from
  AFAD's TDTH portal (§10).

**For the model.** Wall and bench edges that run N60°W or E–W follow natural weaknesses. Expect
broken, irregular faces and abandoned pits along fault zones, and clean, straight, saw-cut faces
away from them.

**Faber quarry section.** It shows two major travertine cycles separated by river deposits, under
river-lake deposits. The composite section is about 121 m: 78 m of outcrop from the quarry floor
to the hilltop plus a 15 m borehole below the floor. The skull came from a block of the Upper
Travertine, which was the only one being quarried in 2002.

**Heritage note.** The site is internationally known as the oldest hominin fossil in Turkey (the
Kocabaş cranium). This may matter for an architectural project about the landscape.

## 7. Stone appearance (for materials and renders)

- **Colours:**
  - white, whitish-yellow and beige; honey colour in vein travertine;
  - grey or greyish-beige on weathered, old terraces;
  - darker brown and porous in the top waste layer (reed type);
  - Fe/Mn staining near faults.
- **Banding:**
  - wavy bands 3–4 cm thick containing millimetre bands, white alternating with reddish;
  - in veins, 1–2 cm bands, darker toward the walls;
  - textures: banded, banded with intraclasts, pisolitic (pea-size spheres), porous (regular or
    concentric) and massive.
- **Saw-cut faces.** Wire- and chain-saw faces are smooth, flat and straight, with visible horizontal
  bedding lines. Dark lines are clay or paleosol bands (10–60 cm) or marl layers.
- **Chemistry (Kocabaş).** CaO 53.5–55.2 %, MgO 0.23–0.51 %, Fe₂O₃ 0.11–0.29 %, loss on ignition
  42.6–43.2 %, i.e. nearly pure calcite [Özpınar et al. 2001, TR].

## 8. How the quarries are worked

- **Methods.** Diamond wire saws plus **chain (arm) saws**. In one Denizli quarry the vertical and
  back cuts are by wire and the horizontal floor cut by chain saw. At a Kaklık quarry, a Demmak
  DZK 3400 chain saw first makes the vertical cut along the bench. Then 10 cm horizontal holes are
  drilled under the bench and 20–25 kW wire saws make the other cuts.
- **Cut sizes at that Kaklık quarry.**
  - **Cutting height ~3.8 m**, cut length **80–90 m**, arm length 4.3 m;
  - output ~50,000 m³ of blocks per year; average block 2.0 × 1.6 × 1.5 m;
  - no water inflow;
  - **~30 m of waste from the surface to the floor**.

  [Çalışkan & Yavuz 2019, TR]
- **Arm reach.** Chain-saw arms reach up to 9 m and are recommended for benches over 6–7 m; cutting
  depths of 3.5–7.2 m are reported [same source].
- **Block yield.** Rose from 7–14 % with wire saw only to 65–80 % with a chain saw (DZK-3400 trials in
  Kaklık–Kocabaş) [same source, citing Demirel et al.]. Large waste volumes are typical, so **dumps
  and block yards are a major part of the landscape**.
- **Bench geometry.**
  - Set by site conditions and machine capacity;
  - 3.5–9.3 m (beige stone) and 5.1–9.4 m (yellow travertine) measured in two quarries outside
    Denizli [Dilmaç & Görgülü 2019, TR];
  - Denizli quarries are summarised as usually 6–8 m with 80–90° slopes (search summary of a 2011
    announcement and an encyclopaedia entry; primary documents not checked).
- **Planning.** Özpınar et al. (2001) [TR] note that quarries "work with today's technology (wire
  cutting) but without forward planning", and that lateral changes in rock type make planning hard.
  This explains the patchwork of irregular pits of different depths seen in the photos.

## 9. Climate, water and caves

- **Climate (Denizli, MGM 1957–2025).**
  - About 566 mm rain per year (sum of monthly means): wettest January (90 mm) and December (89 mm),
    driest August (11 mm) and July (15 mm).
  - Mean temperature about 16 °C (January 6 °C, July 27.5 °C); winter minima below −10 °C.
  - Expect summer-dry, winter-wet runoff, with ponding on flat quarry floors in winter.
- **Groundwater.** The travertine formed from CO₂-rich hot springs rising on faults; the source rocks
  are the Mesozoic limestones. One Kaklık quarry reports **no water inflow**.
- **Karst.** Paleokarst cavities occur in the travertine. **Kaklık Cave** ("the hidden underground
  Pamukkale"), a travertine cave in the same district, was reported in 2020 to be at risk of
  collapse; large quarries operate around it (news reports). There is also the Haydarbaba sinkhole
  (düden) in Kaklık.

## 10. What this means for Quarry Maker

1. **Bench height.** The local evidence points to **6–8 m benches** (3.5–9.4 m range; ~3.8 m cutting
   lifts at a Kaklık quarry). Our default guess of 9 m is at the top of that range.
   - **Suggestion:** use 7 m as the default, or 6–8 m if you prefer a range.
   - With the photo's median bench spacing of 26 m, 7 m benches imply an overall slope of about
     15°. The DEM shows about 14°.
2. **Faces and floors.** Saw-cut walls are 80–90° and straight; floors are flat and often follow a
   bedding plane. This supports the current carved style (85° walls, flat floor plates), and
   supports straightening walls along the N60°W and E–W joint directions if we go further.
3. **Bench directions in our data.** Our traced lines (92.5 km) are only weakly organised:
   - 30 % of their length runs N0–30°;
   - 28 % runs N90–135° (E–W to NW–SE), which matches the published fault and joint directions;
   - the overall preference is weak (coherence 0.15).

   Pits probably follow local property lines and beds more than one site-wide grid. So snapping the
   whole site to one grid would be wrong; snapping area by area could be right.
4. **Layered walls.** For close-up architectural models, walls could carry bed boundaries:
   - white travertine beds of 5–25 m;
   - marl/clay bands of 0.5–6 m (locally 25–30 m);
   - paleosol lines of 10–60 cm;
   - brown porous reed travertine in the top few metres.
5. **Depth.** The DEM (2011–2015) underestimates today's depth. One quarry reports 30 m from
   surface to floor, and the stack of mineable beds is 40–90 m. Carving a few benches below the DEM
   in active pits is therefore plausible.
6. **Dumps and yards.** Low block yields (7–80 %) mean large waste dumps. Stage 08's BLOCK_YARDS
   layer and the dumps detected in stage 07 are real landscape elements, not noise.
7. **Faults.** Fractured, irregular faces and abandoned pits along N60°W and E–W fault zones; clean
   saw-cut benches elsewhere.
